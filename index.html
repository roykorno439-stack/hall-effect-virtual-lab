Theory: Calibration of a Magnetic Field Using Hall Effect

In a semiconductor sample, when charge flows with current density $\vec{J}$ in a direction perpendicular to the applied magnetic field $\vec{H}$ ($z$-direction), the Lorentz force produces a mutually perpendicular deflection ($y$-direction) on the charge carriers. Both electrons and holes are deflected toward the same surface because of their opposite charges and opposite drift velocities. Due to differences in carrier concentration and mobility, a net charge accumulates on the transverse faces, generating the transverse Hall Electric Field $\vec{E}_n$:

$$\vec{E}_n = R \, (\vec{J} \times \vec{H}) \qquad \text{(1)}$$

where $R$ (or $R_H$) is the Hall Coefficient. Consider a rectangular bar of semiconductor having dimensions $x$ (length), $y$ (width), and $z$ (thickness). When $\vec{J}$ is directed along $X$ and $\vec{H}$ is directed along $Z$, the Hall field $\vec{E}_n$ appears along $Y$, producing a measurable transverse Hall voltage $V_n = E_n \, y$ across the width $y$. Since total current $I = J \, z \, y$:

1. Hall Coefficient ($R$) & Carrier Concentration ($n$)

$$R = \frac{V_n / y}{J H} = \frac{V_n \, z}{I \, H} \quad \left(\frac{\text{Volt}\cdot\text{m}^3}{\text{Amp}\cdot\text{Weber}}\right) \qquad \text{(2)}$$ $$R = \frac{E_n}{J H} = \frac{v H}{q \, n \, v H} = \frac{1}{n \, q} \qquad \text{(3)}$$

At equilibrium, magnetic force $F_m = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{H})$ is balanced by the Hall electrostatic force $F_n = q E_n$, giving $v H = E_n$ (for $\theta = 90^\circ$) and $J = q n v$. The sign of $R$ directly determines the sign of the majority charge carriers $q$ ($+R$ for $p$-type holes, $-R$ for $n$-type electrons).

2. Gaussmeter Principle (Unknown Field Calibration)

$$H = \frac{V_n \, z}{I \, R} \qquad \text{(4)}$$ $$V_n = \left(\frac{R \, H}{z}\right) I \qquad \text{(5)}$$

Once $R$ and sample thickness $z$ are known for a fixed sample current $I$, the Hall voltage $V_n$ varies linearly with the magnetic field $H$, allowing the calibrated Hall probe to act as a precision Gaussmeter.

Experimental Procedure & Mandatory Precautions

PRECAUTION YOU MUST OBSERVE:
  • Before putting power to the electromagnet unit, verify that the Variac knob is at the minimum position ($0.0\text{ A}$, extreme left). Also bring the Variac knob to minimum before switching OFF.
  • Maximum allowable current through the Germanium crystal is $2.5\text{ mA}$ only.

Part 1: Magnetic Field Measurement Using Gauss Meter

  1. Switch on the Gauss Meter with the probe away from any magnetic field. Check zero adjustment on the $\times 0.1$ range ($0\text{–}0.6\text{ kGauss}$) using the Zero Control knob.
  2. Keep the range switch at $\times 3$ ($0\text{–}18\text{ kGauss}$) and insert the probe into the air gap between the pole pieces.
  3. Adjust the electromagnet current (e.g., $0.5\text{ A}$ to $2.5\text{ A}$), step down to the appropriate lower range ($\times 1$ for $0\text{–}6\text{ kGauss}$), and record the flux density $H$.

Part 2 (Stage A): Determination of Hall Coefficient ($R$) & Carrier Density ($n$) — Table I

  1. Connect the widthwise contacts (red wires) to the Hall voltage terminals and lengthwise contacts (green wires) to the current terminals.
  2. With $H = 0$, pass a small current through the crystal to observe the zero-field misalignment potential $V_0$ (due to imperfect opposite alignment of widthwise contacts).
  3. Set electromagnet current to $1.5\text{ A}$ and orient the Hall probe perpendicular to $\vec{H}$ for maximum Hall voltage.
  4. Vary probe current across 5 steps ($0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5\text{ mA}$) for Direct Current $(a)$ and Reversed Current $(b)$ (by interchanging the green banana plugs).
  5. Rotate the sample through $180^\circ$ in the magnetic field and repeat measurements for both current directions to obtain columns $(c)$ and $(d)$.
  6. Compute mean Hall voltage $\bar{V}_n = \frac{|a|+|b|+|c|+|d|}{4}$, plot $\bar{V}_n$ vs. $I$, and determine $R = \frac{\text{Slope}\cdot z}{H}$ and $n = \frac{1}{|R|q}$.

Part 2 (Stage B): Calibration of Unknown Magnetic Field (Gaussmeter Principle) — Table II

  1. Fix the sample current at $I = 0.50\text{ mA}$ throughout.
  2. Vary electromagnet coil current across $0.5\text{ A}, 1.0\text{ A}, 1.5\text{ A}, 2.0\text{ A}, \text{and } 2.5\text{ A}$.
  3. At each coil current, record all 4 readings $(a, b, c, d)$ across current reversal and $180^\circ$ probe rotation, compute $\bar{V}_n$, and evaluate $H = \frac{\bar{V}_n \cdot z}{I \cdot R}$.
